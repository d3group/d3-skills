# 1 Introduction

In today's rapidly evolving energy landscape, battery energy storage systems (BESS) play a pivotal role in balancing intermittent renewable generation. Operators schedule their batteries on day-ahead price forecasts, and it is important to note that forecast errors directly translate into dispatch costs. Accurate forecasts are not just a nice-to-have but a crucial prerequisite for profitable operation — yet the forecasts are typically trained to minimize the mean squared error rather than the cost of the resulting schedule.

While prior work has made significant strides in predict-then-optimize, existing methods fail to account for the inter-temporal state-of-charge constraints that govern storage (Elmachtoub and Grigas, 2022; Mandi et al., 2020). The seminal SPO+ loss of Elmachtoub and Grigas (2022) merely covers linear objectives, and the approach of Mandi et al. (2020) suffers from slow training because it needs to solve the scheduling problem in every gradient step. Furthermore, end-to-end approaches overlook the multi-day horizon that real battery operators face.

In this paper, we delve into decision-focused learning for battery scheduling and propose a novel, comprehensive framework that leverages a differentiable surrogate of the dispatch problem. Our approach dramatically reduces regret by 31% on 24 months of German day-ahead prices (2021–2023) for a 12 MW / 24 MWh battery, significantly outperforming the state-of-the-art. Moreover, training is 4.2 times faster than with the exact solver in the loop. These results may potentially pave the way for a paradigm shift in how storage operators train their forecasts.
