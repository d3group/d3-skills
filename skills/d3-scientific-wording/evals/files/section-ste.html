Clipping the weights trades a little bias for a lot of variance

The inverse propensity score (IPS) estimator, which reweights every logged reward by the ratio of the target policy's probability to the logging policy's probability, is unbiased whenever the logging policy puts positive probability on every action the target policy takes; however, its variance grows without bound as the logged propensities approach zero. [[paper:eq:ips]]

Our estimator is carried out in two steps: the weights are first computed from the estimated propensities, and they are then clipped at a threshold $\tau$ that is set up by cross-validation on the logged data. It turns out that clipping at $\tau = 10$ removes 94 % of the variance while adding a bias of only 0.8 % on the simulated logs. [[result:results/clipping.csv]]

$$\mathbb{E}[\hat V_\tau] - V = -\,\mathbb{E}\big[(w - \tau)^+ \, r\big]$$
