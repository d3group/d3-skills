## 3 Model and results

We consider a contest between two firms that invest in grid flexibility. It is clear that the firm with lower costs always wins — this is the key insight of our model. Firms choose effort $e_i \ge 0$ at cost $c_i e_i$, and the prize $V$ is awarded by a Tullock contest success function $p_i = e_i / (e_1 + e_2)$. We prove using simulations that a unique pure-strategy Nash equilibrium exists.

**Proposition 1.** The equilibrium is unique.

**Proposition 2.** Total effort increases dramatically in $V$.

Our results not only characterize the equilibrium but also reveal deep insights into regulatory design.
