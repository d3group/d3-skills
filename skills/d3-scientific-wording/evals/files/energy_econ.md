## 2 Related literature

The literature on dynamic electricity tariffs is vast and multifaceted. Seminal contributions by Faruqui and Sergici (2010) and Allcott (2011) laid the groundwork, but they merely studied short-run responses in pilot programs and fail to capture long-run habituation. Recent studies have made great progress (Fabra et al., 2021; Harding and Lamarche, 2016); however, they overlook heterogeneity across households.

## 5 Results

Our panel regressions on 2,340 households in Bavaria from 2019 to 2023 clearly demonstrate that time-of-use tariffs cause a 9.4% reduction in peak consumption. The effect is highly significant (p < 0.01) and proves that dynamic pricing is the key to a successful energy transition. Moreover, households with heat pumps reduce peak demand by 14.1%, which is a game-changer for grid operators.
