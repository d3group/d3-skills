notes for ICIS 2026 abstract (completed research paper, track "Human-AI Interaction", abstract max 150 words per the call)

- phenomenon: managers rely on decision dashboards; dashboards get simpler (fewer numbers, cleaner visuals)
- our claim: visual simplicity removes cues that make data uncertainty visible ("scrutiny cues") -> managers accept flawed figures more often
- theory: dual-process theory (System 1 / System 2), processing fluency (Alter & Oppenheimer 2009)
- construct we introduce: scrutiny cues = cues that make data uncertainty, provenance and verification needs salient at the point of decision
- study: online experiment, N = 184 managers (Prolific, management experience >= 2 years), between-subjects, simple vs detailed dashboard, 12 decisions each, 4 of the 12 figures were flawed (stale or wrong)
- result: acceptance of flawed figures 64% (simple) vs 41% (detailed), difference significant (logistic mixed model, p < .001); perceived usability higher for simple (5.9 vs 5.1 on 7-pt scale)
- no effect on decision time (n.s.)
- contribution: explains why usability-optimized dashboards can lower decision quality; design implication: keep scrutiny cues even in simple designs
- limitation: online panel, monetary incentive flat
